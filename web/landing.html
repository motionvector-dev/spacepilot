The cheapest machine
is usually the wrong one.

Every scheduler you have used sorts by price. That is the wrong sort. Weights already sitting in memory answer now; the same weights on better silicon spend a minute arriving. Nothing else in your stack knows which of your machines is warm. This does, and it is the only thing it sells.

Wan 2.1 T2V 1.3B, one five-second clip. The 5090 is faster silicon and free to run, and it still loses — fourteen seconds of nothing happening is worse than zero. Sort that column by price and you would have picked it.

score = price + (cold ? load_seconds × value_of_latency : 0)

That is the entire ranking. value_of_latency is a dial you own: at zero it buys the cheapest seconds available, turned up it stops making you wait. There is no second scoring pass you cannot see, and no sponsored tier.

The uncomfortable slide

Thirty-four model variants.
Two numbers we measured ourselves.

2of 34 measured

Most of this registry is other people's arithmetic, and we would rather print that on the homepage than let you find out later. Provenance sits at the same weight as the number it qualifies — never a tooltip, never a footnote, never grey. An instrument that flatters you is a decoration.

measured

We ran it. On this machine. On a date you can read. Two of thirty-four, and the count only goes up by working.

declared

Their number, cited and dated. Measured on a machine that is not yours, under conditions nobody wrote down.

huggingface-api

Summed from the Hub. Real bytes, honestly added up, and still not a measurement.

estimated

A rule applied to a parameter count. The note names the rule. This is most of the registry and we are not hiding it.

Measured · speech 4.6× realtime

Kokoro 82M through ONNX. 12.5 seconds of speech from one sentence in 2.7 seconds of wall clock, M1 Max, nothing else running.

Measured · image 10.52 GB

FLUX.2 klein-4B at 4-bit through MFLUX — the working set we observed, not a figure derived from parameter count.

And when a call fails, you get the failure. Not a plausible number wearing its clothes.

The fleet

Not local versus cloud.
Ships, and one of them is rented.

Local-versus-cloud is a distinction that mattered when you owned one machine. You own four. They find each other over your own tailnet, they keep their own crews warm, and the rented one is just the ship with a meter running. No port is opened. We never hold your identity, and we never see your work.

Where it is today

Early, and saying so
where you can see it.

Works now

Probing, and the real ceiling rather than the advertised one. Compatibility across 34 variants. Runtime install that shows every package it would move before it moves one. Speech, locally, at 4.6× realtime. An MCP server, so an agent can fly this without ever opening a window.

Not yet

Video and image generation on your own silicon. Scheduling across more than one ship at a time. You will not find any of it above, written as though it already worked. Resisting that is the product.
